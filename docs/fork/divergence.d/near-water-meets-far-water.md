### Near water meets the far water
- Files: `crates/host/src/shader_overrides.rs` (`NEAR_WATER_FADE_PROGRAM`, `near_water_fade`), `crates/host/src/fork_graphics.rs`, `crates/host/src/lib.rs`, `crates/d3d9-wgpu/src/shader_override.rs` (`Spec::patch`, `names_naming`), `crates/d3d9-wgpu/src/sm/wgsl.rs` (`convert_patched`), `crates/d3d9-wgpu/src/device.rs`
- Upstream approach: as the game, near (placed) water drawn with a depth map stays as see-through as its water type makes it (`FogAmount` below 1) up to the edge of the loaded cells, where the far (LOD) water, which shows nothing of what lies under it, begins: a lake seen from above shows a line there, strongest with a water mod that makes the water clear
- Fork approach: under the graphics setting `water.near_fade` (next start; off by default and under `original` and `performance`, on under `modern`) the game's own water pixel shaders that read a depth map are drawn with a patch override: converted as always, with their opacity (`FogColor.w`) rising to 1 and their depth falloff reading every depth as deep between 4096 and 8192 units across the ground from the eye. With the setting off no override is set and the device is what it was
- Decision: keep
- Reason: the maintainer wants a lake seen from above to read as one surface with any water mod (nv-rs#897), with nothing specific to one place or mod
- Evidence: `a_patch_keeps_the_body_and_passes_the_device_s_constants_through_the_program`, `a_patch_override_changes_only_the_constants_it_patches`, `names_are_picked_by_the_constants_every_package_s_shader_names`, `the_near_water_fade_patches_the_water_s_opacity_and_depth_falloff`, `the_near_water_fades_in_modern_and_not_otherwise_unless_chosen`; Lake Mead, Hoover Dam and Colorado River pictures with Natural Waters and the plain game (pull request)
